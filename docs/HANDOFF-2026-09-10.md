## 1. 4.5 is unmet, and a third seed is why

**PLAN 4.5 wants three families at 0.80 on the structural split. Two reach it.**
This section briefly said three, on two seeds each. A third seed withdrew one of
them.

| family | structural (frozen `holdout_v3`) | seeds | floor | route |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| `deliver` | **0.89** (0.84–0.94) | 3 | 0.01–0.11 | ordinary PPO |

| `repair_belt` | 0.17 / 0.13 | 2 | not measured | BC (PPO scores 0.00) |
| `build_line` | 0.00 | 1 | not measured | BC (0.981 expert-action accuracy) |

**`keep_line_running` does not hold at 0.80.** A third seed, run after the
first two and through the same code path on the same frozen 100, returned
**0.22** — Wilson [0.15, 0.31], disjoint from seed 1's [0.79, 0.92] and seed
2's [0.88, 0.97]. Its `seeds` and `val` rows are unremarkable (0.88 / 0.77),
so training did not fail; only the structural row collapsed, and the three
rows do not correlate across seeds (seed 2 scored 0.59 on `seeds` and 0.94 on
`structures`; seed 3 scored 0.88 and 0.22). Mean across three seeds is ~0.68.

Two seeds would have published a family at 0.87–0.94 that a third seed shows is
a coin toss. **Two seeds is not enough for a mastery claim in this benchmark**,
and that is the most transferable lesson here.

Every row above carries `holdout_v3`, `start_index 3000`, `covers_frozen_set:
true`, content hash `432c2c4d…`. The hash being identical across families is
the cross-check that the frozen plan was actually applied rather than merely
requested — see §3.

**Superseded by `docs/AGENTIC_ROADMAP-2026-09-10.md`.** That document rules
that 4.5 stays open under its existing requirements, that PPO, imitation and
hybrids are reported separately rather than reinterpreted as mastery

The seed-3 result above is that reconciliation, and it settles the question

## 3. Six defects, one pattern — the most useful thing in this handoff

Every defect found since the last reconcile is the same shape: **something
declared that nothing reads.**

| # | defect | consequence | commit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `clone_expert --holdout` was declared and never read | every BC number was scored on the run's own scenes and published as frozen — the same unpaired comparison that got 4.4 withdrawn | `fa44c79` |
| 2 | `episode_indices` hardcoded to `range(eval_episodes)` | a false provenance claim for exactly the row whose provenance matters, and wrong indices fed to the disjointness check | `b85dbf2` |
| 3 | `RegisteredTask.solve` had never been read | authoring a task required editing framework internals — an R6 gate clause | earlier; same pattern |
| 4 | a docstring promised a disruption check that did not exist | prose asserting a guarantee the code did not provide | earlier; same pattern |
| 5 | `clone_expert` published rates with **no random floor at all** | the two families that might close 4.5 cannot be quoted | `57b2d63` |
| 6 | `factoriorl evaluate` had no `--holdout` | the one supported way to score a provided checkpoint could not reproduce a single published number; it generated its own scenes | `57b2d63` |

**Recommended for the next redirection:** a mechanical countermeasure, not more
vigilance. A test that walks every `argparse` parser in `tools/` and
`src/factoriorl/cli.py` and asserts each declared argument is referenced
somewhere in its module would have caught #1 and #6 at the moment they were
written. #1 alone invalidated a day of published BC results. This is cheap and
it addresses the failure mode that has cost the most rework in this
repository's history.

A second, weaker candidate: a check that any evidence file claiming a frozen
holdout carries a `content_hash` that resolves.
`tests/unit/test_release_provenance.py` already knows how to do this; it is not
applied at write time.

## 4. A negative result, recorded as one

`beyarkay/factorion` distinguishes **disjunctive** tasks (many solutions,
partial progress counts) from **conjunctive** ones (every component
simultaneously right or no reward). It looked like it explained the sharpest
unexplained result here — BC 1.00 on `restore_power` against 0.13–0.17 on
`repair_belt`.

**It does not.** `tools/solution_fragility.py` measures the property rather than
declaring it; excess fragility across five families spans **0.00–0.04** while
the outcomes span **0.00–1.00**. Every family measures disjunctive. Full entry
in `docs/LEDGER.md` ("An idea borrowed from Factorion that did not survive
contact with our data"), evidence in `docs/evidence/r5-solution-fragility.json`.

Two points a redirection should carry forward:

- **The first two numbers were artefacts, and controls caught both.** Without a
  no-op control arm the ranking came out backwards. The control showed that
  nearly all of `deliver`'s apparent fragility was the cost of *spending a
  step*, and that every control failure was injected at the solve's last
  action. The measure was ranking how each solver was written, not the tasks.
- **One observation is recorded but explicitly not claimed.** The column that
  tracks learning outcomes is reference *solve length* — 14.8 actions → BC 1.00,
  16.8 → 0.89, 21.7 → 0.15, 240.0 → 0.00. n=5, no controlled variation, horizon
  confounded with action-type count and with demonstrations per pair. If a
  redirection wants a hypothesis to test, this is the one on the table; it must
  be tested on new families rather than re-fitted to these five.

## 7. Operating notes

  concurrent jobs compete for the same worker ports and RCON endpoints.
